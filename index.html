I am a research engineer at Huawei Hong Kong Research Center, working on generative AI. I currently focus on full-duplex spoken dialogue models, developing synthetic dialogue data and post-training methods for full-duplex interaction. My broader work spans generative modeling, representation learning, and efficient inference.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the University of Hong Kong, supervised by Prof. Ngai Wong and Prof. Edmund Y. Lam. My earlier research explored efficient neural representations and inference; collaborative work also spans GUI agents and multimodal evaluation.

01 / RESEARCH

Questions behind the work

My research spans generative models, AI agents, and efficient computation. Full-duplex spoken dialogue models are the current focus of my work.

01

SPOKEN DIALOGUE · CURRENT FOCUS

Learning when to listen and respond

Full-duplex dialogue requires more than recognizing speech. I work on synthetic dialogue data and post-training for turn-taking, backchannels, and interruptions, adapting a turn-based model for continuous interaction.

Spoken dialogue · Synthetic data · Post-training

02

GENERATIVE MODELING

Representing sound for generation

My music codec work brings together self-supervised encoders, vector quantization, and generative decoders. I have led a team of four engineers and researchers exploring these components for AI music generation.

Neural audio codecs · Flow matching · Representation learning

03

EFFICIENCY & INFERENCE

Making useful models less expensive

From compact neural representations to LLM decoding, I study how model design and inference algorithms affect computational cost. My work spans activation sharing, lookup tables, low-bit quantization, and parallel and speculative decoding.

Neural fields · Compression · Inference acceleration
